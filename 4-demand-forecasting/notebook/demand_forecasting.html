Demand Forecasting Analysis¶

Analyze historical sales patterns to forecast future demand and better understand purchasing trends over time.

Business Goal: Support inventory planning and operational decision-making by predicting future product demand and identifying sales trends.

3. Data Cleaning¶

Standardize column names, then:

  1. Remove non-product lines: postage, carriage, manual adjustments, fees, samples, and gift vouchers.
  2. Remove order lines cancelled in full within 24 hours (same customer, product, and quantity). These are order-entry mistakes, not real sales: for example, an order for 80,995 paper craft sets was cancelled 12 minutes later.
  3. Keep only valid sales rows (positive quantity and unit price). This removes returns, cancellations, and price adjustments.

# 2) Order lines cancelled in full within 24 hours (same customer, product, and quantity).
#    These are order-entry mistakes, not real sales.
def remove_same_day_cancellations(df, invoice, customer, product, qty, date, price):
    is_cancel = df[invoice].astype(str).str.startswith("C")
    purchases = df[~is_cancel].dropna(subset=[customer]).reset_index()
    cancels = df[is_cancel].dropna(subset=[customer]).reset_index()
    cancels[qty] = -cancels[qty]
    pairs = purchases.merge(cancels, on=[customer, product, qty], suffixes=("", "_cancel"))
    pairs["gap"] = pairs[date + "_cancel"] - pairs[date]
    pairs = pairs[(pairs["gap"] >= pd.Timedelta(0)) & (pairs["gap"] <= pd.Timedelta(hours=24))]
    # Match each cancellation to its nearest earlier purchase, one to one
    pairs = pairs.sort_values("gap").drop_duplicates("index_cancel").drop_duplicates("index")
    pairs["value"] = pairs[qty] * pairs[price]
    pairs["minutes_to_cancel"] = (pairs["gap"].dt.total_seconds() // 60).astype(int)
    return df.drop(index=pairs["index"]), pairs

8. Model Accuracy Evaluation (Holdout Test)¶

The forecasts above are next-month projections with no ground truth to score against. To measure accuracy honestly, we hold out the last 3 months as a test set, refit on the earlier months, and compare walk-forward one-step-ahead forecasts for both models using MAPE, RMSE, and MAE.

Caveat: only 12 complete months are available (9 train / 3 test), so these numbers are illustrative, not statistically robust.

In [11]:
# Model accuracy on a held-out test set (proper train/test split)
N_TEST = 3  # hold out the last 3 months
train, test = ts.iloc[:-N_TEST], ts.iloc[-N_TEST:]
print(f"Months available: {len(ts)} | Train: {len(train)} | Test: {len(test)} "
      f"({test.index.min():%Y-%m} -> {test.index.max():%Y-%m})")

def _metrics(actual, pred):
    actual, pred = np.asarray(actual, float), np.asarray(pred, float)
    mae = np.mean(np.abs(actual - pred))
    rmse = np.sqrt(np.mean((actual - pred) ** 2))
    mape = np.mean(np.abs((actual - pred) / actual)) * 100
    return mape, rmse, mae

# Moving-average baseline: one-step-ahead = mean of the prior 3 actual months
ma_preds = [ts.iloc[i - 3:i].mean() for i in range(len(ts) - N_TEST, len(ts))]

# ARIMA(1,1,1): walk-forward one-step-ahead, refit at each step.
# On this short series, statsmodels warns that its default starting values are
# non-stationary or non-invertible and starts from zeros instead; the fit still
# completes, so only those start-value warnings are silenced.
arima_preds, history = [], list(train)
for actual in test:
    with warnings.catch_warnings():
        warnings.filterwarnings("ignore", message="Non-(stationary|invertible) starting")
        fit = ARIMA(history, order=(1, 1, 1)).fit()
    arima_preds.append(fit.forecast(steps=1)[0])
    history.append(actual)

ma = _metrics(test.values, ma_preds)
ar = _metrics(test.values, arima_preds)

eval_results = pd.DataFrame(
    {"MAPE_%": [ma[0], ar[0]], "RMSE": [ma[1], ar[1]], "MAE": [ma[2], ar[2]]},
    index=["Moving Average (3-mo)", "ARIMA(1,1,1)"],
).round(2)
print(eval_results)
print(f"\nARIMA vs baseline -> MAPE {(ma[0]-ar[0])/ma[0]*100:+.1f}%, "
      f"RMSE {(ma[1]-ar[1])/ma[1]*100:+.1f}%, MAE {(ma[2]-ar[2])/ma[2]*100:+.1f}%")

9. Key Insights & Business Recommendations¶

Key insights

  • Monthly sales stayed around £0.5–0.75M from January to August 2011, then rose sharply from September to £1.45M in November 2011.
  • For December 2011, the 3-month moving average forecasts £1.19M and ARIMA(1,1,1) forecasts £1.38M. The gap of about £192K reflects the recent upward trend: ARIMA follows it, while the moving average smooths it out.
  • On the 3-month holdout (September–November 2011), ARIMA's error was about 20% lower than the baseline (MAPE 24.64% vs. 30.66%), and it was better on RMSE and MAE too. Both errors are still high, and with only 12 monthly points the result is illustrative.

Business recommendations

  • Use the two forecasts as a range: the moving average as a conservative lower bound and ARIMA as a growth-oriented upper bound for purchasing.
  • Compare actual sales with the forecast every month, and re-check accuracy as more data comes in.
  • Next steps: forecast weekly or by product category to get more data points, and add seasonality and promotion effects.
